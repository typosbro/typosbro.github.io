Instrument Focus

Hear one player inside a song.

Separate a finished mix into its instruments, then push one forward and pull the rest back. Nothing is re-recorded. You are hearing the original performance, balanced differently.

The channel list is measured from the audio, not assumed. That is why the two songs show different instruments. A song with no guitar simply has no guitar fader.

How to use it

  1. Open a song. Click an instrument name at the bottom, or drag its fader.
  2. The chosen instrument gets +9 dB, the others −6 dB, then the whole mix is levelled to −16 LUFS, so you are judging balance rather than volume.
  3. BALANCED puts everything back flat. M mutes, S solos, keys 1–N solo each channel, Space plays.

How it works

Each song is separated into six stems with HTDemucs-6s: drums, bass, vocals, piano, guitar and a catch-all. Each stem is measured, and any stem below −45 dBFS is reported as absent rather than offered as a fader that does nothing. The page then rebuilds the mix live in the browser from those six tracks. Everything is inside one file; there is no server and no network request after the page loads.

Honest limits

Separation is a best guess, not a clean take. Soloing one instrument can sound hollow where the model dropped a transient, and there is some bleed between channels. other is a bucket, not an instrument — strings, synths and organ all land there. Six channels is this model's ceiling. The audio is encoded at 96 kbps, which costs about 1 percent of the energy above 8 kHz.

Both songs here are copyrighted recordings, embedded for demonstration. The tool itself only rearranges audio the listener already has.
