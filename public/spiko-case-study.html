Three problems worth your time

AI grading at a price a student can pay

The recording goes to a queue; a Durable Object owns the job for that student, with TTL and cleanup so abandoned sessions stop costing money. When the model is slow or over budget, a fallback still returns a usable score. The constraint was never model quality — it was cost per session. A student in Uzbekistan pays in sums, so the margin has to survive inference, platform fees and tax. Closing that loop is what makes the product a business instead of a demo.

Money across three providers

Three payment rails, three webhook shapes, three sets of retry semantics, three settlement lags. Subscriptions are a state machine, webhooks are processed idempotently, and a recovery job picks up the payments that arrive late or twice. Most of the engineering is in the failures: the double charge that should never happen, the subscription that should not silently expire, the user who should not lose access because a callback got lost.

4x growth without a rewrite

The API is versioned and the mobile client can be forced to a version, so a breaking change can ship without waiting for the app store. Feature flags gate what a cohort sees. That is what let the backend move fast while the old clients stayed alive — the backend held an error rate under 0.01% through the growth period, and the rewrite everyone expects never became necessary.

What I would do differently

  • Instrument before shipping. I added real telemetry late and paid for it in guesses. The next product gets an event catalogue and a cost dashboard on day one.
  • Test the money paths first. Payment flows got tests after the second incident, not before the first.
  • Smaller modules, earlier. I spread logic across layers because it looked professional. It made the mental model expensive. Fewer, deeper modules would have shipped the same product faster.
